Document Purpose

This document defines the role, purpose, boundaries, and relationships of the six primary Quick Tools in the Mantra Self-Love ecosystem.

The purpose is to ensure users immediately understand:

What each tool does
When they should use it
What they can expect after opening it

And internally, to ensure we don't create overlapping or disconnected features.

1. Information Architecture Overview
   The Six Quick Tools
   Quick Tool User Intent
   Mood Check-In “How am I feeling right now?”
   Reset Now “I need help feeling better right now.”
   Understand Feelings “Help me understand what I'm feeling.”
   What Helps Me “What can I do that might help?”
   My Journal “I want to express or reflect.”
   Know Myself “I want to understand myself better.”

These are entry points, not necessarily single activities.

Each Quick Tool can contain multiple connected experiences.

3. Quick Tool #1 — Mood Check-In
   Primary User Question

“How am I feeling right now?”

Role in the Ecosystem

Mood Check-In is the daily starting point.

It helps the user pause and notice their current emotional state without immediately trying to fix it.

Primary function

Awareness before action.

What it includes
Core Daily Check-In

The main experience:

Identify broad emotional state
Identify a more specific emotion
Rate intensity
Add optional context
Receive a brief personalized response
Potential future additions
Daily emotional snapshot
Mood history
Weekly emotional patterns
Check-In streak
Shareable emotional reflection
What Mood Check-In should NOT become

It should not become:

A long psychological assessment
A therapy session
A detailed journaling experience
A giant Emotion Wheel

The goal is:

Fast enough to do daily, meaningful enough to learn something.

Ideal duration:

30–90 seconds

4. Quick Tool #2 — Reset Now
   Primary User Question

“I need something that can help me right now.”

Role in the Ecosystem

Reset Now is for immediate emotional regulation.

The user may not want to understand their emotions deeply.

They may simply feel:

Overwhelmed
Anxious
Angry
Stressed
Unable to focus

And want immediate support.

Core experience

The user chooses what they need.

For example:

I want to...
Calm down
Stop overthinking
Feel grounded
Release tension
Focus
Take a break

Mantra immediately recommends a short activity.

Activity types
Breathing
Box breathing
Slow breathing
Guided breathing
Grounding
5–4–3–2–1
Sensory grounding
Body
Quick body scan
Release tension
Stretch/reset
Mindfulness
Cloud watching
Short guided mindfulness
Present moment exercise
Core rule
Fast access. Minimal friction.

The user should be able to open the tool and start something useful within seconds.

What Reset Now should NOT become

It should not become:

A massive meditation library
A course
A long educational experience

Think:

Emergency drawer of helpful tools—not a content library.

5. Quick Tool #3 — Understand Feelings
   Primary User Question

“What exactly am I feeling, and why?”

Role in the Ecosystem

This is the emotional intelligence and emotional awareness hub.

Unlike Mood Check-In:

Mood Check-In

“What am I feeling right now?”

Understand Feelings

“Let's explore and understand this feeling.”

This distinction is important.

Core activities

1. Emotion Wheel

Users can explore:

Broad emotions
Specific emotions
Relationships between emotions

Example:

Anger
↓
Frustration
↓
Irritation
↓
Resentment 2. What & Why

A guided activity:

What am I feeling?

↓

What might be contributing to this?

↓

What happened?

↓

What thoughts are connected to it?

3. Emotional Equation

A structured framework for understanding:

Situation

- Interpretation
- # Personal context
  Emotional response

The goal is to help users understand that emotions often have context.

4. Depth of Emotion

Explore whether an emotion has something underneath it.

Example:

Anger
↓
Frustration
↓
Feeling unheard
↓
Fear of not being valued

This should be exploratory—not presented as automatically diagnosing the user's “real” hidden emotion.

Future personalized use

If a user repeatedly checks in as anxious:

Understand Feelings can intelligently surface anxiety-related exploration.

6. Quick Tool #4 — What Helps Me
   Primary User Question

“What can I do when I feel like this?”

Role in the Ecosystem

This is the user's personal coping discovery system.

The important distinction:

Reset Now

Help me right now.

What Helps Me

Help me discover what works for me over time.

Core Experience

The user can answer something like:

What are you going through?
Feeling anxious
Feeling low
Overthinking
Angry
Stressed
Lonely
Unable to focus

Then Mantra recommends appropriate options.

The system learns

After trying something:

Did this help?

The user's answer becomes personalization data.

Over time:

My Toolkit
Things that often help you when you're anxious
Going for a walk
Breathing
Writing things down
Things that help when you're overwhelmed
Breaking tasks down
Taking a short break
The long-term goal

Build a deeply personalized:

My Coping Toolkit

Not:

"Here are generic coping tips."

But:

"Here are things we've learned tend to help you."

7. Quick Tool #5 — My Journal
   Primary User Question

“I want to express what's on my mind.”

Role in the Ecosystem

The Journal is the user's private reflection space.

It should support both:

Free expression

Write whatever is on your mind.

and

Guided reflection

Answer a meaningful prompt.

Core experiences

1. Free Write

A distraction-free writing space.

2. Guided Prompts

Personalized prompts based on:

Recent mood
Emotions
Current challenges
Previous insights

Example:

"You mentioned feeling overwhelmed recently. What's taking up the most space in your mind?"

3. Reflection Prompts

Examples:

What happened today?
What stayed with you?
What do you need right now?
What would you tell yourself with kindness? 4. Personal Insights

Over time, the journal can help surface themes and patterns—carefully and without pretending to clinically analyze the user.

Future potential
Story of Me

Selected user insights from journaling and self-exploration can eventually contribute to their evolving personal narrative.

Only with appropriate privacy and user control.

8. Quick Tool #6 — Know Myself
   Primary User Question

“Who am I, and what matters to me?”

Role in the Ecosystem

Know Myself is the deepest self-exploration area.

This is not primarily about solving today's emotion.

It's about long-term self-understanding.

Core Activities

1. Getting to Know Me

A guided self-discovery journey.

Users gradually explore:

Experiences
Personality
Values
Interests
Strengths
Challenges

The experience can build progressively rather than asking everything at once.

2. My Story

Based on user inputs over time, Mantra helps create an evolving narrative.

Example concept:

Your story isn't finished. This is simply where you are right now.

The story evolves as users share more.

3. My Mission Statement

Users explore:

What matters to me?
What kind of person do I want to become?
What do I stand for?

Eventually creating a personal statement.

4. Exploring Inner Conflict

Helping users explore competing desires.

Example:

"I want success, but I'm afraid of failing."

The goal is not to provide a simplistic answer but to help the user examine both sides.

12. Home Screen Behavior

The Quick Tools should remain stable and understandable.

But the experience after opening them should become personalized.

This is important.

❌ Don't constantly rename and reorder everything based on algorithms.

That makes the interface confusing.

Instead:

Stable entry points

Users know where things are.

↓

Personalized experiences inside

The content adapts based on the user.

13. Recommended Order of Quick Tools

For the current home experience:

1. Mood Check-In

The primary daily habit.

2. Reset Now

Immediate value.

3. Understand Feelings

Emotional awareness.

4. What Helps Me

Practical coping discovery.

5. My Journal

Reflection and expression.

6. Know Myself

Deep self-exploration.

This order follows:

Notice → Respond → Understand → Discover → Reflect → Explore

One thing to note: Understand Feelings and What Helps Me could potentially switch depending on how the home UI prioritizes immediate utility, but the recommended order above is a strong starting point.

14. Product Design Principle for Every Tool

Every Quick Tool should answer three questions immediately:

1. Where am I?

The purpose is obvious.

2. What can I do here?

The next action is obvious.

3. Why would I come back?

There is continuity, progress, personalization, or a useful outcome.

    Final Architecture Principle

The six Quick Tools should feel simple on the surface.

But underneath, they should form one intelligent ecosystem:

Check in with yourself → Understand yourself → Find what helps → Reflect → Discover who you are.

The user should never need to understand the entire system.

They simply open the tool that matches what they need right now.

Meanwhile, Mantra quietly connects the journey in the background.
